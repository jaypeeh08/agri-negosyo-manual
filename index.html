Getting started

For everyone: signing in, finding your way, and setting up a new store.

@@CH1@@Chapter 1

About Agri Negosyo POS

Agri Negosyo POS runs the daily work of an agricultural supply store: selling at the counter, keeping stock by the sack and by the kilo, buying from suppliers, taking client bulk orders, delivering them, and knowing at any time how the business is doing.

1.1How the program is set up

  • One computer is the store server. It keeps every record and starts by itself when it is switched on, even before anyone signs in.
  • Every other device only needs a web browser. Cashiers and staff open the program on the store computer, or on any phone, tablet or laptop connected to the store's Wi-Fi. Nothing is installed on those devices.
  • No internet needed for daily work. Selling, stock, orders and reports all work offline. The internet is used only for activation, program updates and the cloud add-ons.
  • Records are safe. The program makes checked backups by itself, and you can make your own at any time (chapter Backup, updates and activation).

1.2Who uses which part

WhoSigns in atUses
Owner, Admin/adminEverything: products, prices, stock, suppliers, clients, orders, deliveries, reports, staff, settings
Cashier/cashierThe POS, their cashier session and transactions; releasing orders and running delivery trips

1.3Words used in this manual

WordMeaning
SRPThe selling price of a unit (suggested retail price).
Capital priceWhat the store pays for a unit: its cost. Used for stock value and profit.
Base unitThe unit a product is bought and counted in, usually the biggest one: SACK, BOX, BOTTLE.
Selling unitAnother way the product is sold, for example by the KG from a 50 kg sack.
SessionA cashier's shift, from counting the cash drawer at the start to counting it at the end.
Bulk orderA client's order, entered by staff and released from stock at pickup or delivery.
BatchOne delivery of a medicine or vaccine with its own batch number and expiry date.
Main store / BranchThe main store holds the central inventory, suppliers and client orders; a branch sells from its own stock.

2.3Forgot password

A cashier who forgot their password asks the owner, who sets a new one in Users (chapter Staff accounts and the audit log).

If the owner or an Admin forgot theirs, the developer helps without needing the internet:

  1. On the sign-in page press Forgot password?
  2. Type the Username and press Get request code.
  3. Send the request code that appears (it starts with PW-) to the developer by text or Messenger.
  4. The developer replies with an access code like PZG0H-FCHJN. Type it in Access code and press Continue.
  5. Type the new password twice (at least 12 characters) and press Save new password. Then sign in with it.
i
Note: The access code works only for that request, for one hour, and in the same browser. After five wrong codes, start again with Start over. This works only on an activated program.

2.4The screen layout

  • Menu (left). Pages grouped by work: Overview, Clients, Deliveries, Selling, Inventory, Purchasing, Reports, Administration. You see only the pages your account may use.
  • Top bar. Where you are (for example Home / Selling / Cashier POS), today's date, your name, and Sign out.
  • Bottom bar. The activation status (for example Activated), the program version and the developer.
  • Blue bar across the top. A program update is coming: finish the sale and close the cashier sessions (chapter Backup, updates and activation).
  • On a phone the menu opens from the button at the top.
✓
Tip: Lists have a search box at the top. Typing filters the list at once; there is nothing to press.

2.5Signing out

Press Sign out at the top right whenever you leave the counter or the computer, so nobody can work under your name.

@@CH3@@Chapter 3

Setting up a new store

This chapter is for the day the program is installed. Do the steps in this order; most take only a few minutes.

3.1Day-one checklist

  1. Install the program on the store computer and choose Main store or Branch (done by the developer).
  2. Set up the store: business name, address and the owner's account (below).
  3. Activate the program (below).
  4. Fill in the business profile: phone and TIN for receipts (chapter Settings).
  5. Add the staff accounts for the cashiers (chapter Staff accounts and the audit log).
  6. Check the categories and units: they come ready; add any the store needs (chapter Categories, brands and units).
  7. Add the products with the stock on the shelves through Opening Products (chapter Opening Products).
  8. Add suppliers and clients, with any balance or advance deposit carried over from the old records.
  9. Make a backup in Backup & Restore, and let each cashier sell one test item and cancel it.

3.2Store type: Main store or branch

When the program is installed, Setup asks whether this computer is the Main store or a Branch.

OptionChoose it when

BranchThis is another store of the same business that sells from its own stock and receives stock from the main store.

The activation must be for the same kind of store; the choice cannot be changed later without reinstalling. After activation the store takes its number and name from the license (the Main store is B1, Branch 2 is B2). That code starts every receipt number, for example INV-B1-20261101-0001.

@@CH4@@Chapter 4

The cashier session

Every sale belongs to a cashier session: your shift, from counting the cash drawer at the start to counting it at the end. You cannot sell until your session is open.

4.1Opening your session

  1. Open Cashier Session in the menu (or the POS asks for it when you open it).
  2. Count the cash in the drawer.
  3. Type it in Opening cash and press Open session.
FieldWhat it is forExample
Opening cash (₱)The cash already in the drawer before the first sale (the change fund). Count it; do not guess.2,000.00

The POS header now shows when your session opened, how many sales you made and their total.

4.2Closing your session

At the end of your shift, after the last sale:

  1. Open Cashier Session.
  2. Count all the cash in the drawer.
  3. Type it in Counted cash and press Close session.
FieldWhat it is forExample
Counted cash (₱)All the cash in the drawer now, counted. The program compares it with what should be there.14,350.00

The program works out the expected cash: the opening cash plus the cash from your completed sales. The difference (over or short) is saved with the session for the owner to review.

i
Note: Gcash, Maya, bank transfer and check payments are not in the drawer, so they are not part of the cash count. They are listed by method on the Transactions page.

4.3Your earlier sessions

The list below shows your sessions with their opening, expected and counted cash and the difference. Search by session number, date (for example 2026-10-23) or open / closed. Press a session to see its transactions.

5.2Finding a product

Type in Product search / barcode: part of the name, the product code, the old code, the brand, or scan the barcode with a scanner.

Each product shows its prices per unit and its stock the way it is kept, for example 9 SACK · 50 KG: nine sealed sacks and one opened sack with 50 kg left. One sack is always counted as the opened one, so ten full sacks show as 9 SACK · 50 KG.

5.3Adding a product to the sale

Press Add on the product. A window opens:

  • Products sold in one unit (a bottle, a piece): choose the unit button, set the quantity with − and + or type it.
  • Products sold by the sack and by the kilo have two boxes:
BoxWhat to typeExample
SACKWhole sacks. Typing a decimal splits it: 1.5 becomes 1 SACK + 25 KG by itself.2
KGLoose kilos from an opened sack.25

How the price is worked out:

  • 1 sack and 25 kg is charged as 1.5 sacks at the sack price.
  • Kilos alone (no sack) are charged at the kilo price.
  • The window shows the total before you add it, and the most you can sell (Up to …). The program never sells more than the stock, counting what is already in the sale.

Press Add to sale.

5.4Changing or removing a line

Tap the line in the current sale. The same window opens: change the unit or quantity and press Save, or press Remove to take it out.

✓
Tip: The search you typed stays after adding, so you can add the next item from the same list.

@@CH11@@Chapter 11

Opening Products

When the store starts using the program, its products and the stock already on the shelves go in through Opening Products (Inventory menu). This is the move from the manual records to the program. It is not a purchase or a restock: there is no supplier and nothing is owed.

!
Important: Opening Products is available for 3 months from the day the store was set up. The page shows the last day and the days left. After that it leaves the menu: products are added in Products, and their stock comes in through Restock.

11.3Good to know

  • Each product takes its opening stock once, before any of its stock moves.
  • Opening stock is valued at the product's capital price and recorded in the stock history as Opening stock.
  • Count before the store opens, or after it closes, so nothing is sold while you count.
@@CH12@@Chapter 12

Stock alerts and expired stock

Stock Alerts shows the stock that needs attention.

PartWhat it listsWhat to do
Low stockProducts at or below their reorder level.Order more, then receive it with Restock.
ExpiringBatches that expire within 90 days, soonest first.Sell these first; move them to the front of the shelf.
ExpiredBatches past their expiry date that still hold stock.Remove them from the shelf and press Write off.

Search by product name or code.

12.1Expired batches

Expired stock is never sold: the POS skips expired batches and sells from the good ones, and it will not sell more than the good stock.

To take an expired batch out of the stock, press Write off on it. The stock goes down by what was left in that batch, and the write-off is recorded in the stock history and the audit log with the batch number and expiry date.

i
Note: Only a batch that has expired can be written off here.

Agri Negosyo POS runs one store completely on its own. When the business grows, two add-ons let it grow with you, without changing how anyone works and without reinstalling: the developer switches them on and they arrive by themselves.

Add-onPriceWhat you get

22.1Why an extra branch

  • One business, one view. The main store sees every branch: its stock, its sales, its cashier sessions, its transfers, from the main store's own screen.
  • Stock where it is needed. Send stock to a branch in a few clicks; the branch confirms what arrived. Nothing gets lost between stores.
  • Prices you control. The catalog and prices are managed at the main store; branches sell at the same prices.
  • Every record knows its store. Receipt numbers carry the store's code (INV-B2-…), so the stores never mix up their sales.

  • Your records are not only on one computer. A computer can break down, be stolen, catch a virus, or be damaged by a flood or a fire.   • Nothing is lost while offline. Without internet, changes wait in a queue on the store computer and are sent as soon as the connection is back.
  • Branches share automatically. The catalog and prices go from the main store to the branches, transfers and stock levels go both ways, and each branch's sales reach the main store.
  • Selling never waits for the cloud. The counter always works at full speed; the cloud work happens in the background.

@@CH25@@Chapter 25

Cloud backup and the cloud package

The cloud package keeps the store's records safe off the store computer and lets the stores of one business share their records. It comes with Multi-store.

FunctionWhat it does
Cloud backupEvery change is copied to the cloud, encrypted, as it is made: sales, products, stock, clients, payments, deliveries.
Branch sharingThe catalog and prices from the main store, transfers, the stock at every branch, and each branch's sales and cashier sessions to the main store.

Selling never waits for the cloud. Without internet, changes wait in a queue and are sent when the internet is back; nothing is lost.

25.1What reads and writes are

The cloud counts two kinds of work, the way a meter counts electricity:

KindWhat it isWhen the program does it
WriteSending one record to the cloud.Cloud backup: each record that is added or changed is one write. Branch sharing: each record a store shares with the others.
ReadGetting one record from the cloud.Branch sharing: each record a store receives from the others (new prices, transfers, a branch's sales at the main store).

For example, one counter sale with 3 products is saved as a few records (the sale, its 3 lines, the payment, the stock movements), so it uses about 8 to 10 writes. A store with 150 sales a day, plus its deliveries and restocking, uses roughly 2,000 to 3,000 writes a day, well inside the allowance. A branch receiving the main store's new prices reads one record per product changed.

i
Note: The program's own check with the developer (activation, updates, the functions for the store) is not counted: it is part of the program, not of the cloud package.

25.2The daily allowance and the over-limit price

Included every day, for each storeAbove the allowance
Reads20,000₱5 per 1,000 reads
Writes15,000₱10 per 1,000 writes

The allowance starts again every day. Most stores never go above it. Above it, cloud work continues and is charged at the prices above, for example 2,000 writes over the allowance in one day cost ₱20. Above a daily hard stop (set by the developer), cloud work pauses until the next day; selling and everything on the store computer keep working.

25.3Checking the cloud

Settings → Cloud connection shows whether the cloud is connected, how many changes are waiting, when the stores last shared, and the usage meter for today and this month.

The Activation page shows today's reads and writes against the allowance, and any amount over it.

  • The amount due shows on the Activation page and at the developer.
  • If an amount due stays unpaid, the developer may turn the cloud off until it is paid. Selling and everything on the store computer keep working; changes wait and are sent once it is back on.

25.4What stays on the store computer

The audit log never goes to the cloud. The sample practice store is never uploaded. Records are encrypted before they leave the store computer.

  • Receive supplier deliveries in Restock when they arrive, from the delivery receipt.
  • Release pickup orders and record deliveries as they happen.
  • If a blue update bar appears, finish the sale and close the session when it asks.

27.3Closing the store

  1. Each cashier counts the drawer and closes their cashier session.
  2. The owner checks Transactions → By date for the day: totals, discounts and payment methods.
  3. Record the day's expenses.
  4. Leave the store computer on, or shut it down normally; the records are saved as they are made.

27.4Every week

  • Download a backup and keep it on a USB drive away from the store computer.
  • Check Stock Alerts and Forecasting to plan supplier orders.
  • Review client balances on Clients and follow up overdue ones.

27.5Every month

  • Print or download the sales, stock and expense reports for the month.
  • Check Users: turn off accounts of staff who left.
  • Run System check in Settings; give any code to the developer.
